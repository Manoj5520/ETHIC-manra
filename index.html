03 / Discovery philosophy

Search for forces — not annoyances.

An annoyance describes what someone notices. A force-first investigation asks why the behaviour keeps recurring, what the user does to compensate and whether a real residual gap survives those workarounds.

Do not begin with an invention.

Begin with a repeated human behaviour in its real context. Let the evidence determine whether a product should exist.

Signal before solution
Evidence before commitment

Recurring friction

What visibly goes wrong? How often, in what context, and for whom? Avoid turning one incident into a universal problem.

04 / Visual model

The unofficial second interface.

A simple visual model for explaining why a workaround is useful evidence — but not, by itself, proof that a new product is needed.

A / FIRST INTERFACE

The existing object

The product or environment is intended to perform a job. In the real context, it may not fully control a force, fit or movement.

›
B / SECOND INTERFACE

The human workaround

The user adds a tie, clip, wedge, hand, stack or improvised repositioning. The adaptation is behavioural evidence.

›
C / RESIDUAL GAP

What still fails

The remaining failure may justify further research. It may also be adequately solved by a cheap workaround or an existing product.

What a workaround tells us

There is a job someone is trying to get done.

Observe what was modified, the conditions that trigger the modification, how often it happens and what the user considers an acceptable result.

What a workaround does not tell us

That a business opportunity is validated.

The workaround could be sufficient, rare, cheap to tolerate, or already addressed by a solution the observer has missed. Interview, benchmark and test before concluding.

External precedent · Polygons Design · Fold-flat measuring spoons

Six measures. Two tools. Flat when stored.

Conventional measuring-spoon sets use several separate pieces. Polygons reconfigures two flexible, flat tools: marked pinch points form three volumes on each tool, then the spoons flatten again for compact storage. The design changes the relationship between measurement capability, physical form and storage rather than creating a wholly new utensil category.

6 → 2Six conventional measures represented by two fold-flat tools, according to company materials and coverage.
2.8 mmReported thickness when flattened. This is a published specification, not independently measured here.

What this precedent demonstrates

  • A mature, familiar object can still contain a specific use or storage constraint.
  • A change in geometry and interaction can compress several functions into a smaller physical system.
  • The visible simplicity of the final object can conceal substantial iteration and manufacturing work.

  • One successful precedent does not prove demand for any unrelated ETHIC concept.
  • Reported sales are not profit, margin, repeat purchase, audited accounts or evidence of ETHIC's traction.

Scientific lens — what a proper evaluation would measure

For a measuring tool, relevant tests include volumetric accuracy at every marked size, repeatability across users, fold-cycle fatigue, cleanability, material/food-contact compliance and storage retention. 01

Observe the friction

A cable mount may move or detach when a cable is pulled, often at an angle. Capture real examples, context and frequency before generalising.

Working hypothesis: repeated side-load, leverage, adhesive/surface interaction or mounting geometry may transfer load in a way the attachment cannot tolerate.

Inspect the workaround

Look for users pressing, re-sticking, re-routing, adding clips or changing the mount. Record what they do and the conditions that make it recur.

10 / Evidence discipline

Keep the language as rigorous as the research.

Every claim should reveal how it is known, what remains uncertain and what evidence would change the decision.

Tier 01 / Signal

Observed

A direct, documented real-world occurrence. Record context, conditions, date/source where possible and what was actually seen — without adding causal assumptions.

Example: a specific mount moved during a documented pull test.
Tier 02 / Interpretation

Hypothesis

A reasoned explanation or proposed mechanism that still needs testing. State what would support it and what would disprove it.

Example: angled cable force may increase peel loading at the base.
Tier 03 / Supporting proof

Verified

A claim supported by repeatable testing, measurements, direct user evidence or reliable external sources appropriate to the claim.

Example: a documented test series under defined conditions produces a repeatable result.

Language that preserves truth

  • ≠Observed does not mean proven.
  • ≠Interesting does not mean qualified.
  • ≠Qualified does not mean validated.
  • ≠Validated does not mean ready to scale.

What a good evidence record contains

  • 01The exact claim being tested.
  • 02Source, context, method and conditions.
  • 03Result, limitations, repeated trials or conflicting evidence.
  • 04The decision the evidence supports — and what it does not.
11 / Prototype economics

A bounded experiment, not a manufacturing budget.

The current planning assumption is a small prototype that answers a specific physical question. It is not a claim that funds are secured, a build has started or the complete commercial product can be manufactured at this cost.

Target prototype budget
₹11,500–₹12,500Planning envelope: approximately ₹11k–₹13k

Working budget assumption for a prototype-scale experiment. Final cost depends on scope, materials, fabrication method, iteration count and vendor quotation.

Budget decision rules

Spend to answer a question.

  • Define the failure mode and acceptance criteria before paying for a prototype.
  • Prefer the simplest test rig or mock-up that can falsify the physical hypothesis.
  • Separate proof-of-principle costs from tooling, certification, inventory and production setup.
  • Get current quotations for fabrication/materials; do not treat indicative vendor estimates as committed prices.
  • Do not release the prototype budget until the research question, test method and P1 gate are clear.
Exploratory build options

Materials are test variables — not a bill of materials.

Methods discussed for early physical exploration include 3D-printed test geometry (including TPU where its properties fit the question), silicone casting for an appropriate interface, and NdFeB magnets only if a magnetic attachment is justified by the actual mechanism. None is selected or performance-tested.

First decide what the bench test must prove; select materials afterward. Avoid adding electronics or complex mechanisms before they are necessary.

Fabrication search area

Locations to investigate — not confirmed suppliers.

Potential places to seek quotations or prototyping capability include Kurnool, Hyderabad (including T-Works as a resource to investigate), Hosur and Bengaluru.

No supplier, partnership, slot, quotation or manufacturing route is represented as secured. Reconfirm availability, capability, lead time and cost directly before making a decision.

Prototype gate: keep the ₹11,500–₹12,500 target as a bounded experiment envelope. It does not include assumed production tooling, certification, inventory or launch costs; do not spend it until the test question and acceptance criteria are written.

13 / Conditional roadmap

The next 90 days — only after qualification.

This is a proposed sequence for planning, not an announced schedule or evidence that work has already begun. Its clock should start only when a candidate and test objective are clearly defined.

Phase 01 / Days 1–30

Observe & define

Goal: establish the problem and physical mechanism well enough to choose a useful test.

  • Collect direct observations and user context.
  • Record current workarounds and existing alternatives.
  • Define measurable bench-test conditions.
  • Review close prior art and disconfirming evidence.
Phase 02 / Days 31–60

Prototype & falsify

Goal: test whether the simplest physical principle reliably addresses the stated failure.

  • Build within the bounded prototype envelope.
  • Run repeatable tests with recorded conditions.
  • Document failures as well as successful trials.
  • Revise or kill the concept based on the result.
Phase 03 / Days 61–90

Validate & decide

Goal: find out whether people value the outcome and whether an initial business case survives scrutiny.

  • Conduct real user/problem validation.
  • Test willingness to pay with defined methods.
  • Model landed costs, channel and likely margins.
  • Decide: continue, revise, pause or stop.

The sequence may take longer or stop early if evidence contradicts the hypothesis. Time targets must never override product safety, research quality or the stop criteria.
